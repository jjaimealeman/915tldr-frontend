## Why this is a checkpoint

This is a `costly` decision by 03-CONTEXT.md's own rating, not a one-way one — moving the render
step after Phase 4 has built against it means rewriting the build/deploy pipeline and re-timing
the render budget, which is expensive but not a migration. A `costly` rating alone would earn a
flag, not a gate.

The gate is here for a different reason: **the decision is genuinely unmade.** D-01 deferred it
to measurement on purpose, the measurements now exist, and nobody has chosen. This is not
re-asking a question the owner already answered — it is asking the one they deliberately
postponed until there was something to answer it with.

    Where does the render step run: inside the existing cron Worker, in a separate Worker fed by
    Queues, or in CI?
  </decision>
  <context>
    03-CONTEXT.md D-01 deferred this to three measurements rather than choosing up front, and
    gave its reason: Phase 2 had two confident estimates collapse under measurement — a published
    `~$7.44` backfill that re-derived to `$80.01–$144.13`, and a D-16 "dead source row" that did
    not exist at all. Both were plausible and both were wrong.

    The measurements are now in `docs/phase-03/measurements.md` and 03-06's summary. **Present
    them here, as measured, before the options** — the figures are the substance of this
    checkpoint and the executor must fill them in from the document rather than paraphrasing:

    - Cron-trigger CPU ceiling, measured empirically at this project's 2-hour interval, and
      whether `limits.cpu_ms` moved it.
    - Existing production cron CPU consumption per invocation (p50/p95, over the stated window),
      and therefore the headroom.
    - Per-page render cost (p50/p95), broken into D1 read, render and manifest write, plus the
      separately-measured fixed build-startup cost.
    - D1 REST pagination p50/p95 at the live corpus row count.
    - The full-corpus projection at p50 and at p95, `fixed + rows × per-page`, compared against
      the ceiling and against the headroom.
    - Whether STATE.md's carried-forward 300-second figure was confirmed or corrected, and by
      how much.

    That last point may change the shape of this decision entirely. STATE.md's blocker reasons
    that "a full 82k rebuild at ~4 ms/page is ~330 s, over the 300 s Worker CPU ceiling
    regardless of location, so Queues fan-out may be required." 03-RESEARCH.md found that
    Cloudflare's own limits table gives Cron Triggers an interval-dependent ceiling — 30 seconds
    under a 1-hour interval, 15 minutes at 1 hour or longer — and this project's cron runs every
    2 hours. If the measured ceiling is 15 minutes rather than 300 seconds, the "Queues fan-out
    may be required" conclusion was reached from a figure roughly 3x too low, and option A may be
    comfortably viable where it previously looked impossible.

    Note also that a full-corpus rebuild is the worst case, not the normal one: the manifest's
    content hash (03-04) exists precisely so the steady-state render is only new and changed
    articles. Weigh both — the steady-state cost and the worst-case rebuild — because they can
    point at different answers.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="option-a">
      <name>Inside the existing cron Worker</name>
      <pros>
        No new infrastructure, no new deployment, no queue to reason about. The cron already runs
        every 2 hours and already knows what changed. Simplest possible operational story and the
        fewest moving parts to debug — which is the other half of this phase's goal.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Shares a CPU budget with RSS fetch, summarisation and dedup, so render cost competes with
        pipeline cost in one invocation, and a slow render degrades ingestion. Bounded by the
        measured cron ceiling with no way to exceed it. Couples the public site's build to the
        pipeline Worker's lifecycle, which Phase 12's cutover would then have to untangle.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-b">
      <name>A separate Worker fed by Queues</name>
      <pros>
        Fan-out: each consumer invocation gets its own CPU budget, so a full-corpus rebuild is not
        bounded by a single invocation's ceiling. Isolates render cost from pipeline cost
        entirely. Retries and dead-lettering come from the platform rather than hand-rolled.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        The most infrastructure of the three: a queue, a producer, a consumer, and their failure
        modes. Partial-batch failure becomes a real state to reason about, and a build that is
        partly complete is exactly the "which layer is lying" confusion this phase exists to
        remove. Only worth its complexity if the measured numbers genuinely rule out the others.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-c">
      <name>In CI</name>
      <pros>
        No Worker CPU ceiling at all, and no CPU billing for render. Full Node available, so the
        Astro build runs in its native environment rather than a constrained one. Matches
        03-RESEARCH.md's Architectural Responsibility Map, which places the render step's primary
        tier in build tooling/CI.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Depends on a CI pipeline whose existence 03-03 had to establish rather than assume —
        confirm what that resolved to before choosing this. Cron-driven CI invocation is a
        different trigger mechanism than a Cron Trigger and may need separate setup. Ties the
        render cadence to CI scheduling rather than to the pipeline that produces the content.
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select: option-a, option-b, or option-c — or describe a hybrid, e.g. steady-state in one and full rebuild in another.</resume-signal>
</task>
