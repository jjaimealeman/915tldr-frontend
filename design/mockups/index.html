Congress Holds Leon Black in Contempt Amid Epstein Investigation

The House of Representatives has held billionaire investor Leon Black in contempt for failing to comply with subpoenas related to the investigation of Jeffrey Epstein. This decision, which follows a unanimous vote by the House Oversight Committee, now sends the matter to the Justice Department for potential criminal prosecution. Black's refusal to provide documents and testify has raised concerns among lawmakers, particularly regarding a $180 million payment he made to Epstein. Black's legal team is contesting the subpoenas, claiming they are illegitimate and an abuse of power, while lawmakers argue that accountability is essential for Epstein's survivors. The situation underscores the ongoing scrutiny of high-profile individuals connected to Epstein's network.

Key Details:

  • House held Leon Black in contempt on Wednesday.
  • The case is now with the Justice Department for possible prosecution.
  • Black is accused of refusing to comply with subpoenas for documents and testimony.
  • Lawmakers are pressing for answers about a $180 million payment to Epstein.

Trump Retains Colombia's Drug Certification Status Amid New Leadership

President Donald Trump has decided to keep Colombia on the list of countries that have failed to meet U.S. drug control obligations, a designation it received a year ago. This decision comes as Colombia's new president, Abelardo de La Espriella, has committed to an aggressive anti-drug campaign. Trump indicated that if Colombia makes significant progress in dismantling narcoterrorist networks, he may reconsider its status in the future. The U.S. also recognizes Mexico's efforts against drug trafficking but emphasizes the need for further action. Meanwhile, Venezuela has been removed from the descertification list due to improved cooperation against drug cartels under its interim government.

Key Details:

  • Colombia remains on the U.S. descertification list for drug control as of 2025.
  • President Abelardo de La Espriella has pledged to combat coca cultivation and cocaine production.
  • Mexico is urged to enhance efforts against narcoterrorist organizations.
  • Venezuela's status has changed due to improved cooperation against drug trafficking.

New Study Confirms T. rex Was Warm-Blooded, Changing Our Understanding of Dinosaurs

Recent research has confirmed that Tyrannosaurus rex had a body temperature of approximately 97 degrees Fahrenheit, indicating it was warm-blooded. This finding, published in the journal Science Advances, is based on chemical analysis of fossilized teeth and adds to evidence that T. rex could thrive in colder climates. The study enhances our understanding of the dinosaur's behavior, physiology, and ecological interactions during the Late Cretaceous period, around 69 to 66 million years ago. Researchers believe this new method could be applied to other dinosaur species, potentially reshaping our views on their metabolic rates and lifestyles.

Key Details:

  • T. rex body temperature estimated at 97°F (36°C), similar to humans.
  • Study published in Science Advances provides direct evidence of warm-bloodedness.
  • Research utilized fossilized teeth from a T. rex skeleton at the Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County.
  • Technique could be applied to other dinosaurs to explore their metabolic rates.

EPISD Raises Tax Rate, Impacting Homeowners in El Paso

The El Paso Independent School District (EPISD) board has approved a new tax rate that will increase the average homeowner's tax bill by approximately $114 annually. The new rate is set at $1.0745 per $100 of taxable property valuation, which is slightly below the maximum rate allowed by voters. This change raises the average tax due on homes from $1,179 last year to $1,294.18 this year, reflecting a rise in average taxable home values from $109,167 to $120,455. Homeowners should be aware of exemptions available, including a $140,000 exemption for most homeowners and additional exemptions for seniors and disabled individuals.

Key Details:

  • New tax rate approved at $1.0745 per $100 of property valuation.
  • Average homeowner's tax bill increases from $1,179 to $1,294.18.
  • First $140,000 of property value is exempt from school taxes for most homeowners.
  • Additional $60,000 exemption for seniors and disabled individuals.
  • Veterans with 100% disability rating are exempt from all property taxes.
